IF THERE IS ONLY ONE THING YOU TAKE AWAY FROM OWNING A SIG SAUER P322, IT IS THIS: LOAD THE 20-ROUND MAGAZINE ONE CARTRIDGE AT A TIME UNDER CONSTANT SPRING TENSION.

The P322 is one of the best-handling rimfire trainers ever built — full-size ergonomics, 20+1 capacity, optics-ready, suppressor-ready, and an exceptionally crisp enclosed single-action trigger. Almost every reliability complaint new owners encounter comes down to one simple habit: do NOT pull the side thumb-assist tabs all the way down and dump rounds in loosely. Because .22 LR has a protruding rim in a double-stack magazine, dropping rounds loosely causes rimlock and nose-diving. Pull the thumb tab down just enough for one round at a time, slide it cleanly rearward against the spine, and gently tap the back of the magazine every 5 rounds. Master that 10-second habit and your P322 will run like clockwork.

GOLDEN RULE 01 // AMMO & BORE Feed quality 40gr copper-plated high-velocity ammo (e.g., CCI Mini-Mag) for cleanest cycling. Inspect & bronze-brush the bore every 200–300 rounds — cheap unplated lead accelerates barrel leading.
GOLDEN RULE 02 // REAL CONTROLS It uses an enclosed internal SAO hammer (not a striker). The thumb safety only engages when cocked, you can rack the slide on Safe, and standard models have NO magazine disconnect.
GOLDEN RULE 03 // TAKEDOWN QUIRK Before field-stripping, you must unscrew the 1/2×28 thread adapter (or suppressor) and install the flush barrel cap — otherwise the slide cannot clear the fixed 4-inch barrel.

SECTION II — MECHANICAL ARCHITECTURE & CONTROL INTERLOCKS

PARAGRAPHS 2-1 THROUGH 2-4 // FACT-VERIFIED

EXECUTIVE BRIEF: Despite resembling SIG's striker-fired P365/P320 family externally, the P322 is a ground-up fixed-barrel straight-blowback pistol with an enclosed Single-Action-Only (SAO) internal hammer. Its lightweight anodized aluminum slide lets it cycle reliably even with a micro red-dot optic installed.

2-1. REAL-WORLD CONTROL BEHAVIOR (TRY IN 3D!)

  • Manual Thumb Safety (Ambidextrous): Push UP for SAFE, DOWN for FIRE. Mechanically blocks the trigger bar and only engages when the internal hammer is cocked. The slide can still be racked with safety ON.
  • Slide Catch Lever (Ambidextrous): Push UP while slide is rearward to lock open; push DOWN (or pull slide back & slingshot) to release. Automatically locks open on an empty magazine — does not auto-lock with no magazine inserted.
  • Visual Chamber Check (No Protruding Red Pin): Standard P322 pistols do not use a rear protruding pin. Verify chamber status by retracting the slide slightly (press-check) to observe the brass rim directly inside the open ejection port.

2-2. CRITICAL SAFETY & DRY-FIRE NOTES

WARNING — NO MAGAZINE DISCONNECT (STANDARD MODEL)
Removing the magazine from a standard SIG P322 DOES NOT prevent a chambered round from firing. Always remove the magazine FIRST, then retract the slide and visually & physically verify an empty chamber. (Only specific state-compliant SKUs add a magazine disconnect.)

Dry-Fire Protocol (Manual p. 69): While SIG designed the P322 as a dry-fire trainer, the official Operator's Manual recommends inserting and rotating the supplied orange Chamber Safety Flag 45° into dry-fire position to cushion firing pin travel and eliminate long-term pin wear.

PARAGRAPH 2-3 // WHY FIXED BARREL + ALUMINUM SLIDE MATTERS FOR NEW OWNERS

Unlike centerfire 9mm pistols (P320/P365) that use a tilting Browning locked-breech barrel, .22 LR operates at lower chamber pressure (~24,000 PSI max) and cycles via unlocked straight blowback. SIG anchored the 4.0-inch carbon-steel barrel rigidly to the stainless-steel chassis and wrapped the recoil spring concentrically around it.

  • Inherent Accuracy: Because the barrel never tilts or shifts between shots, mechanical accuracy is exceptionally consistent — especially with an optic mounted directly to the slide.
  • Optic Weight Tolerance: Historically, mounting a red dot onto a heavy steel .22 LR slide caused sluggish cycling. SIG milled the P322 slide from hard-coat anodized aluminum alloy so adding a compact Shield RMSc / ROMEOZero red dot (~0.5–0.8 oz) keeps slide inertia well within reliable blowback limits.
  • Do Not Ride the Slide Forward: As warned in SIG Manual Section 3.4, guiding the slide slowly forward by hand robs recoil-spring momentum and can leave the breech slightly out of battery. Pull cleanly rearward and release sharply (try both in the 3D Bench above to see the difference!).

SECTION V — STRIP, BORE LEADING & CUSTOMIZATION

PARAGRAPHS 5-1 THROUGH 5-3 // ZERO TOOLS REQUIRED

EXECUTIVE BRIEF: The P322 field-strips in under 10 seconds with zero tools — provided you remember its single hardware quirk: unthread the 1/2×28 suppressor adapter and install the flush barrel cap first. Rimfire powder is inherently dirty; a 3-minute wipe-down and bronze bore brush every 250–300 rounds keeps reliability sky-high.

5-1. OFFICIAL 5-STEP STRIP PROCEDURE

  1. Point muzzle in a safe direction; press magazine catch and remove magazine.
  2. Retract slide and visually & physically verify chamber is completely clear.
  3. If installed, unthread suppressor & 1/2×28 adapter and thread on the factory flush barrel cap.
  4. With slide closed, rotate left-side takedown lever 90° counterclockwise (up/left).
  5. Retract slide fully rearward, lift rear of slide UP off chassis rails, and guide forward off the fixed barrel.

5-2. BORE LEADING & INCLUDED FACTORY UPGRADES

  • Bore Leading Check: After your first 100 rounds, inspect the barrel throat/rifling with a flashlight. If you notice dull grey/silver lead streaks, soak with bore solvent and pass a .22 bronze brush (a quick dry bore-snake pass alone won't break heavy lead!).
  • Included Flat & Curved Triggers: Every P322 ships with both a flat-faced (installed) and curved polymer trigger shoe — swap in minutes with a small pin punch (try toggling it live in Section I!).
  • Direct Optic Cut: Remove the two top hex screws on the rear sight plate to directly mount any compact Shield RMSc / SIG ROMEOZero micro red dot — zero adapter plates needed.
PARAGRAPH 5-3 // RECOMMENDED LUBRICATION POINTS (LIGHT OIL ONLY!)

Because .22 LR combustion produces heavy unburnt wax and carbon grit, over-oiling a rimfire pistol turns lubricant into grinding paste. Apply sparingly:

  • Stainless Chassis Rails: 1 tiny drop of synthetic CLP per rail, wiped thin.
  • Fixed Barrel Exterior / Recoil Spring: Light film only to prevent surface rust on the carbon-steel barrel.
  • Breech Face & Extractor: Keep DRY! Brush carbon out from beneath the extractor hook with a nylon brush so it grips .22 LR rims positively.
  • Enclosed Hammer Pivot: 1 micro-drop on hammer pivot pin every 500 rounds.

OPERATOR NOTE — HOLDOVER IS NOT A DEFECT

A 25-yard zero means the bore is angled slightly up relative to the sight line. At 100 yards a 40-grain Mini-Mag has fallen about 13.6 inches below that line, so the front sight must be held high by ~13 MOA. The AUTO HOLDOVER button solves it; the ELEV ↑ / ELEV ↓ buttons move in 2-MOA steps. Note also that a 660 fps Quiet-22 will not cycle the P322 slide at all, yet its bullet still flies — with mortar-like drop.

7-4. ACCURACY MICRO-VARIATION & SIGHT OBSTRUCTION

WHY EVERY GROUP IS DIFFERENT

  • Gaussian dispersion: Box-Muller normal deviations in both axes, scaled by ~2.1 MOA × the load's accuracy factor. Stinger and Quiet-22 open up; Standard Velocity is the tightest.
  • Bore leading: the live fouling percentage from Section V adds group size. Run the bore brush to restore accuracy.
  • Wind: 0 / 5 / 10 / -10 MPH crosswind uses the classic drift = wind × (t - vacuum time) approximation.
  • Breathing sway: a slow two-axis sine sway only appears while actually looking through the sights.
  • SIM-AUTO burst: progressive muzzle climb and widening dispersion per successive shot.
  • Obstructed sights: with no sight picture the group opens dramatically — the model refuses to pretend you can aim through a suppressor.

8-1. DOES A "SWITCH" EXIST FOR THE SIG P322?

SHORT ANSWER: NO.

The P322 is a straight-blowback, fixed-barrel rimfire pistol with an enclosed single-action-only (SAO) internal hammer. Its fire-control group is inside the frame/chassis. Devices commonly called "switches" are associated with striker-fired pistol architectures that use a removable rear slide cover plate; the P322 does not use that architecture. Its removable top plate is an RMSc optics / rear-sight cover, not a fire-control interface.

Accordingly there is no commercially made, legal, drop-in conversion device for this pistol, and the Custom Shop in Section VII contains only lawful, commercially available accessories. The SIM-AUTO [TEST ONLY] toggle is a software-only visualization with a red indicator — not a part, not an attachment, and not something you can buy or add to a gun.

WHY THE ARCHITECTURE MATTERS (AT A HIGH LEVEL)

News reports and ATF / DOJ advisories describe these devices in the context of Glock-pattern striker-fired pistols, where the rear of the slide houses a striker assembly behind a removable plate. The P322 instead uses a hammer-driven, blowback rimfire system with a completely different rear-of-slide arrangement.

That difference is the whole answer to "would one fit?" — different operating system, different fire-control layout, different caliber and slide. No further mechanical detail is provided here, deliberately.

8-2. WHAT PLATFORM ARE THEY NORMALLY ASSOCIATED WITH?

Public law-enforcement advisories most often use the phrases "Glock switch", "machinegun conversion device (MCD)" and "auto sear". The overwhelming majority of reported cases involve Glock-pattern striker-fired semi-automatic pistols; "auto sear" is an older, broader term used for other platforms (notably AR-pattern and AK-pattern rifles and, historically, conversion parts for certain pistols). Federal agencies treat a qualifying device as a machinegun in its own right, whether or not it is attached to a firearm.

TERMINOLOGY NOTE

"Switch" is colloquial journalism shorthand, not a manufacturer's product category. Legally, the relevant term is machinegun as defined in the National Firearms Act. Forced-reset triggers are a separate, heavily litigated category and are outside the scope of this fact sheet.

8-3. WHY THEY APPEAR SO OFTEN IN NEWS & LAW-ENFORCEMENT ADVISORIES

This is context for readers who have never heard of the term — not an endorsement, not an explanation of how to obtain or make anything:

  • Platform ubiquity. Glock-pattern pistols are among the most common civilian handguns in the United States, so any illicit accessory associated with that platform appears disproportionately often in case reporting.
  • Small, cheap, easily trafficked. ATF and DOJ advisories describe these devices as small, inexpensive items frequently imported or shipped through parcel and online channels, which makes interdiction a recurring theme in press releases.
  • Unauthorized 3D-printed polymer copies. Federal cases have involved home-printed polymer versions. DOJ has stated plainly that a conversion device made with a 3D printer is treated the same as a factory-made metal one. No printing details of any kind are included here.
  • Documented recovery growth. A 2023 public advisory from the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of North Carolina cited ATF figures of more than 5,000 conversion devices recovered in the U.S. from 2017–2021, a 570% increase over the 814 recovered in 2012–2016. ATF has also described a large multi-year rise in recoveries nationwide.
  • Agency training & public warnings. DOJ has run officer-training efforts so standard aftermarket parts are not confused with prohibited devices, and U.S. Attorney's offices and ATF have issued repeated public service announcements. That media volume is why the word "switch" reached mainstream headlines.

8-4. FEDERAL LAW & PENALTIES (PUBLIC-RECORD SUMMARY)

AUTHORITYWHAT IT SAYS (PLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY)
26 U.S.C. § 5845(b)
National Firearms Act definition		Defines "machinegun" broadly: not only a weapon that fires automatically, but also any part designed and intended solely for converting a weapon into a machinegun, and any combination of parts from which one can be assembled. ATF's published position is that a conversion device by itself is an illegal machinegun even with no pistol present.
18 U.S.C. § 922(o)
Firearm Owners' Protection Act, 1986		Prohibits civilian transfer or possession of machineguns manufactured after 19 May 1986, with narrow government / licensed-dealer exceptions. Because modern conversion devices postdate that cutoff by decades, there is no civilian registration path.
18 U.S.C. § 924 & 26 U.S.C. § 5871
Penalties		Unlawful possession is a federal felony carrying up to 10 years in federal prison; the general felony fine provision allows fines up to $250,000. Manufacturing, trafficking or importation adds further charges and longer sentences.
18 U.S.C. § 924(c)
Sentencing enhancement		If a machinegun is possessed during a crime of violence or drug-trafficking crime, a 30-year mandatory minimum applies, served consecutively to any other sentence; a second such conviction can carry life. Many states add their own penalties on top.

8-5. WHAT THE RED "SIM-AUTO [TEST ONLY]" BADGE ACTUALLY IS

A label and a timing loop. When armed, holding the burst button repeats the already-existing semi-automatic shot routine at a hypothetical ~1,050 RPM, progressively adds muzzle climb, and widens the simulated group. There is no 3D device, no geometry, no printable file, no part number and no installation step anywhere in this file. 8-6. IF SOMEONE ENCOUNTERS ONE

ATF public advisories advise contacting a local ATF field office to arrange a safe, lawful surrender, and caution against attempting to destroy it oneself. Do not handle, transport, modify or experiment with such an item. Again: this dossier contains no instructions for making, fitting, testing or using one.
